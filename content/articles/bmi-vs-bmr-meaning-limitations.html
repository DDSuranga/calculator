BMI and BMR answer different questions

Body mass index (BMI) relates body weight to height. A basal metabolic rate (BMR) estimate concerns energy use at rest. Neither tells you, on its own, what to eat or whether you are healthy. A daily calorie estimate adds further assumptions about activity and is a third quantity.

BMI calculation and adult categories

BMI = weight in kilograms / (height in metres)2.

For 70 kg and 175 cm, convert height to 1.75 m: 70 / 1.75² = 22.857142…, displayed as 22.9 by the BMI Calculator. Entering centimetres into a metres-based formula would be a unit error.

CDC's categories for adults aged 20 and older are below 18.5 (underweight), 18.5 to below 25 (healthy weight), 25 to below 30 (overweight), and 30 or above (obesity). These are screening categories, not diagnoses. Children and teens require age- and sex-specific interpretation rather than these adult cutoffs.

Near a boundary, the one-decimal display can conceal a difference in the underlying value. For example, 76.5 kg at 1.75 m gives about 24.98, which displays as 25.0, while the tool assigns its category from the unrounded number. Do not infer a medical change from the rounded display alone.

The implemented resting-energy equation

The BMR Calculator uses the simplified Mifflin–St Jeor equation. With W in kilograms, H in centimetres and A in years:

  • Male coefficient: 10W + 6.25H − 5A + 5.
  • Female coefficient: 10W + 6.25H − 5A − 161.

The original study estimated resting energy expenditure in healthy adults aged 19–78. The site's “BMR” label should be understood as a predictive resting-energy estimate, not a laboratory measurement under strict basal conditions. The selector chooses the equation coefficient; it does not measure body composition or individual physiology.

For a 30-year-old, 70 kg, 175 cm adult using the male coefficient: 700 + 1093.75 − 150 + 5 = 1,648.75 kcal/day, displayed as about 1,649. With the female coefficient and otherwise identical inputs, the estimate is 1,482.75 kcal/day, displayed as about 1,483. The difference is built into the equation; it is not an individually measured difference.

Daily energy needs are not the resting number

The Calorie Calculator multiplies the unrounded resting estimate by an activity factor. Its choices are 1.2, 1.375, 1.55, 1.725 and 1.9. These are rough model assumptions, not activity measurements.

Using 1,648.75 and the selected moderate factor 1.55 gives 2,555.5625 kcal/day, displayed as about 2,556. Multiplying the rounded 1,649 would give a slightly different intermediate result. This maintenance estimate is not a prescribed intake, minimum intake or weight-loss target.

When a simple number is not enough

As CDC explains, BMI does not distinguish fat from muscle and bone or show fat distribution. Muscular people and others with different body composition may therefore need a broader assessment. A BMI category alone cannot confirm or exclude a health problem.

These tools are not intended to set nutrition targets for children, pregnancy, breastfeeding or clinical conditions. The energy tools accept ages 19–78 and broad software limits of 20–300 kg, 100–250 cm and BMI 10–70. Passing those limits is not evidence that an estimate is appropriate or healthy. Note that the BMR input range starting at 19 does not change CDC's adult BMI interpretation starting at 20.

Illness, medications, disability, aging and unusual activity patterns can make a simple estimate unsuitable. For an individual assessment or a concern about weight or energy needs, discuss the result and its limitations with a qualified healthcare professional. Do not use it to diagnose a condition or begin restrictive treatment.
