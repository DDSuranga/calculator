Separate the loan payment from the housing budget

A mortgage's principal-and-interest payment is not the entire cost of owning a home. The Mortgage Calculator adds monthly equivalents of the annual property tax and home insurance you enter. Its result is a useful subtotal, not a complete affordability assessment.

Principal is the outstanding amount borrowed. Interest is the charge on that balance. Property tax and homeowners insurance do not repay the loan. The CFPB explains this distinction between principal and interest and the total monthly payment. An escrow arrangement may collect taxes and insurance with the loan payment; paying them separately does not make them disappear from the budget.

Match each input to the formula

First subtract the down payment from the home price: P = price − down payment. The down-payment field takes an amount, not a percentage. Let r be the nominal annual percentage rate divided by 100 and then by 12, and n be the term in years multiplied by 12.

Monthly principal and interest = P × r / (1 − (1+r)−n)

Displayed monthly total = principal and interest + annual property tax / 12 + annual home insurance / 12

At zero interest, principal and interest becomes P/n. These equations assume a fixed rate, equal monthly periods and payments at month-end. The EMI explanation derives the same reducing-balance loan formula. Use the loan's interest rate on the stated basis; an APR that includes other borrowing costs is not automatically the same input.

Worked example: price 300,000, down payment 60,000

Assume a 30-year fixed-rate loan at a nominal 6% annually, property tax of 3,600 per year and home insurance of 1,200 per year. All amounts must use one currency. The current calculator labels monetary outputs “Rs.” and does not convert currencies; the arithmetic below is currency-neutral, not a quote for a particular market.

The first month's interest is 240,000 × 0.005 = 1,200.00. About 238.92 of the loan payment reduces principal; the separate 400.00 for tax and insurance does not. Over 360 payments, unrounded loan arithmetic gives 518,011.65 in principal and interest, including 278,011.65 interest. Holding taxes and insurance unchanged for 30 years gives the tool's “Total Payment” of 662,011.65.

That lifetime total excludes the 60,000 down payment. Adding it gives 722,011.65 before other costs. The tool's label should not be read as the all-in purchase and ownership cost. Real payment schedules may adjust the last payment for rounding.

Costs the result does not automatically include

  • Mortgage insurance, including PMI where applicable. This is different from homeowners insurance; CFPB describes mortgage insurance as protection for the lender.
  • HOA or condominium dues, repairs, maintenance and utilities.
  • Closing costs, lender fees, points, transfer taxes and upfront escrow funding.
  • Future changes in tax assessments, insurance premiums or interest rates.

These items have no separate automatic calculation here. An empty tax or insurance field is treated as zero, not as an estimate based on the property. Obtain actual figures and avoid entering a monthly bill in an annual field.

Check the effect of a changed assumption

If annual property tax rises from 3,600 to 4,800 while the other assumptions stay fixed, monthly tax rises by 100.00 and the displayed monthly total becomes 1,938.92. Principal and interest is unchanged. This is why a fixed-rate loan can still have a changing total housing payment.

Compare the estimate with the lender's documents and local tax and insurance information. Adjustable-rate loans, interest-only periods, balloon payments and unusual repayment schedules need a different model. This guide explains arithmetic and exclusions; it does not recommend a loan or determine what you can afford.
