Cube

V = a³

Measure one edge of the cube.

Rectangular Prism

V = l × w × h

Use three perpendicular dimensions.

Cylinder

V = π × r² × h

Use radius, not diameter, and perpendicular height.

Sphere

V = 4/3 × π × r³

Radius is half the diameter.

Cone

V = π × r² × h / 3

Enter perpendicular height, not slant height.

Conical Frustum

V = π × h × (R² + Rr + r²) / 3

A truncated cone with parallel circular ends. Either radius may be larger.

Ellipsoid

V = 4/3 × π × a × b × c

Enter half of each full axis length, not the full widths.

Spherical Cap

V = π × h² × (3R − h) / 3

R is the parent sphere radius, not the cap base radius. Height must be at most 2R.

Tube

V = π × h × (R² − r²)

Calculates tube material volume, excluding the hollow center. Inner radius must be smaller than outer radius.

From dimensions to volume

  1. Pick the shape and measure the labeled dimensions.
  2. Choose a unit beside each dimension and a result unit.
  3. Calculate, review the result, then Copy or Clear.

Use the right measurements

Radius is half of diameter. Ellipsoid semi-axes are half of the full widths. Heights are perpendicular, not slanted. These models describe ideal geometric solids; irregular objects need a different method.

Examples, conversions and shape limits

A frustum with radii 3 cm and 1 cm and height 6 cm has volume 26π ≈ 81.68 cm³. An ellipsoid with semi-axes 2, 3 and 4 cm has volume 32π ≈ 100.53 cm³.

A spherical cap with sphere radius 3 cm and height 1 cm has volume 8π/3 ≈ 8.38 cm³. A tube with outer radius 3 cm, inner radius 2 cm and length 5 cm contains 25π ≈ 78.54 cm³ of material, not liquid capacity.

1 inch = 2.54 cm and 1 foot = 12 inches. Length conversion happens before calculating volume: a 1 m cube is 1,000,000 cm³. Inputs must be positive. Tube inner radius must be less than outer radius; cap height must not exceed the sphere diameter. Results are rounded to two decimals, with scientific notation for very small values. Unrepresentable results show a validation message.

Formula references: conical frustum, ellipsoid, spherical cap.

Volume Calculator

Calculate volume for cubes, boxes, cylinders, spheres, and cones with clear unit-based results.

Select a shape and enter dimensions to calculate volume.

How to Use

Choose a solid and unit, then enter positive dimensions in that same unit. Use perpendicular height for cylinders and cones, not slant height. Circular inputs require radius, which is half the diameter.

Formula and interpretation

Rectangular prism: length × width × height. Cube: side³. Cylinder: π × radius² × height. Sphere: 4 × π × radius³ / 3. Cone: π × radius² × height / 3. Results use cubic units such as cm³ or m³. Each card converts the dimension units you select into the chosen result unit before calculating volume.

Example

A 10 cm cube has volume 1,000 cm³. A 2 m × 3 m × 4 m rectangular prism has volume 24 m³. A cylinder with radius 2 cm and height 3 cm has volume 12π, approximately 37.70 cm³. A cone with the same radius and height has volume 4π, approximately 12.57 cm³.

Frequently asked questions

What if I know the diameter?

Divide the diameter by 2 before entering the radius. Using the diameter as the radius overstates the result.

How do cubic units convert?

Convert input lengths to a common unit first. One cubic meter equals 1,000,000 cubic centimeters; 1,000 cm³ equals 1 liter.

Can I calculate an irregular object?

These formulas assume one of the five supported solids. Split a composite object into suitable solids and combine their volumes, or use a different measurement method.

Can I calculate CBM for a rectangular box?

Yes. CBM means cubic meters. Choose rectangular prism and m, then enter all three outside dimensions in meters. A 60 cm × 40 cm × 30 cm box is 0.6 m × 0.4 m × 0.3 m = 0.072 m³. Ten identical boxes occupy 0.72 m³ before allowance for packing or stacking. This tool calculates one solid at a time, not freight charges or dimensional weight. For multiple identical cartons, use the CBM Shipping Calculator to enter quantity and dimensions in centimeters, meters, inches or feet.
