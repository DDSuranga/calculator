How to Calculate a Restaurant Tip and Total Bill

Separate the amount you tip on from tax and service charges, and avoid adding charges twice when finding the final bill.

On this page

  • The two calculations
  • Read the receipt before choosing the base
  • Check service charges and automatic gratuities
  • Round the monetary amount, then check the total
  • Before you pay

A tip is a percentage of a chosen amount, while the final restaurant payment may also contain tax or a service charge. Keeping those amounts separate prevents two common mistakes: tipping on a different base than you intended and adding a charge that is already included.

The Tip Calculator takes a bill amount and a tip percentage. It calculates the tip and their sum. It does not identify tax or service charges on a receipt, decide the tipping convention, or split a bill among people.

The two calculations

Tip = chosen tip base × tip percentage / 100
Total of base and tip = chosen tip base + tip

For a $48 bill at 18%, the tip is 48 × 18 / 100 = $8.64. If $48 is the complete amount you owe before the tip, the payment is 48 + 8.64 = $56.64.

Enter 48 as the bill and 18 as the percentage to reproduce that result. The current tool displays a dollar symbol. The percentage arithmetic itself does not convert currencies or determine local customs.

Read the receipt before choosing the base

Illustrative receipt; tax is an example amount, not a local tax-rate claim
Receipt itemAmount
Food and drink subtotal$48.00
Tax$3.84
Amount due before optional tip$51.84

If you choose the $48 subtotal as your tip base, the 18% tip remains $8.64. Your final payment is $51.84 + $8.64 = $60.48. The calculator's $56.64 total for a $48 input does not contain the separate $3.84 tax: you must account for that tax once.

If instead you choose the full $51.84 as the tip base, 18% is $9.3312, rounded to $9.33, and the final payment is $61.17. The difference comes from the chosen base, not a change in the percentage formula.

Check service charges and automatic gratuities

A receipt may already contain a service charge or automatic gratuity. Read its description and ask the restaurant if it is unclear. Do not assume it is absent, or assume every charge has the same meaning across countries and businesses. These examples teach the arithmetic; they do not prescribe a universal percentage or a legal obligation.

If a charge is already in the amount due, adding it again overstates the payment. Only add a further amount if you intend to do so. Use the Percentage Calculator for a standalone percentage, or the Sales Tax Calculator for separate tax arithmetic using a rate you have verified.

Round the monetary amount, then check the total

For a $37.50 base, 15% is $5.625. Using conventional half-up cent rounding gives $5.63 and a $43.13 total. Rounding rules can differ between payment systems; use the final amount shown on the receipt when reconciling a real charge.

For a quick mental estimate, find 10% by dividing by ten and add half of that amount for 15%. The estimate is useful for checking a result, but keep the exact calculation until the final rounding step when recording money.

Before you pay

  • Confirm whether your chosen base includes tax.
  • Check for a service charge or gratuity already listed.
  • Calculate the optional tip once.
  • Add it to the actual amount due, without duplicating tax or charges.
  • Check the rounded final payment.

Adding 18% to a bill is different from measuring how much one bill has changed from another. For that distinction, read Percentage Increase and Decrease.
