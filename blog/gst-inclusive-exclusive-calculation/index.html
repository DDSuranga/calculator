GST Inclusive vs Exclusive: Add and Remove GST Correctly

Choose the correct starting amount and reverse GST without confusing included tax with a discount on the gross price.

  • Removing GST from an inclusive total

Start by identifying what the quoted amount includes

An exclusive amount is the taxable base before the GST being calculated. An inclusive amount already contains it. The GST Calculator provides Add GST and Remove GST modes for these two starting points. Choosing the wrong mode can produce a plausible-looking but incorrect invoice calculation.

Every rate in this guide is hypothetical for arithmetic practice. No example identifies the correct rate for a product, service or transaction. Confirm classification, the supply date, applicable notifications, exemptions and valuation before entering a rate. A rate in the dropdown is not a legal recommendation; Custom permits other inputs.

Adding GST to an exclusive base

For taxable base B and combined GST percentage r:

GST = B × r/100; inclusive total = B × (1+r/100).

Assuming an applicable combined rate of 18%, a base of ₹1,000 gives GST of ₹180 and a total of ₹1,180. Enter 1000, rate 18 and Add GST. The intra-State or inter-State selection changes the displayed tax components, not this total.

Removing GST from an inclusive total

If T already includes that combined rate:

Base = T × 100/(100+r); GST = T × r/(100+r).

For ₹1,180 inclusive at an assumed 18%, the base is 1180 × 100/118 = ₹1,000 and tax is 1180 × 18/118 = ₹180. Enter 1180 and choose Remove GST to reproduce it. The denominator is 118 because the inclusive price represents 118% of the base.

Subtracting 18% of ₹1,180 instead would subtract ₹212.40 and leave ₹967.60. That is a discount on the gross amount, not removal of the included GST. If the base is unknown, divide by the tax multiplier first.

Use the combined rate in the denominator

For an ordinary intra-State example with 9% CGST and 9% SGST, each component within ₹1,180 is 1180 × 9/118 = ₹90. Do not divide each component by 109: both taxes are included in the same gross amount. The approach follows CGST Rule 35 in CBIC's published rules, which uses the sum of applicable tax rates in the denominator.

For an inter-State calculation at a combined 18%, IGST is ₹180. See CGST, SGST and IGST for the classification assumptions and exceptions. Selecting a mode here does not establish the legal nature of a supply.

Applying 18% to the entire ₹3,000 would produce ₹540, which is ₹130 too much for these assumptions. Calculate each independently classified line at its confirmed rate and then reconcile totals. The tool takes one amount and one rate at a time; it does not classify mixed or composite supplies.

Rounding and legal scope

Use full precision for intermediate arithmetic. For ₹999 inclusive at 18%, the unrounded base is about ₹846.610169 and GST about ₹152.389831, displayed as ₹846.61 and ₹152.39. Component and invoice rounding can leave small differences; follow the applicable invoicing and payment rules rather than silently forcing a calculator total onto an invoice.

The tool does not decide taxable value, discount eligibility, input tax credit, registration, reverse-charge liability or filing obligations. It does not model compensation cess or import customs calculations. Entering 0% only gives zero arithmetic tax; it does not establish an exemption or legally zero-rated supply.
